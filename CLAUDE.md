# A Matter of Time — v6.1

This file describes the game **as it is now built (v6.1)**. Don't rebuild from scratch; edit what's here. Earlier versions were replaced by the briefs `v2-update.md` … `v3.6-update.md`, `v4-images.md` and `v5-update.md`, which are kept for history in `archive/briefs/`. Where they disagree with this file, this file is current. `docs/DECISIONS.md` records every judgment call made while building v5 through v6.1 and why. `docs/roadmap-notes.md` holds ideas for later versions. **Before editing `site/content/story.md`, read `docs/content.md`; before editing `site/js/`, read `docs/engine.md`** (they were the nested `content/CLAUDE.md` and `js/CLAUDE.md`, which no longer load on their own).

**v5 made the story engine responsive.** Cards are no longer hand-wired to each other: every card says when it can appear, and the engine draws from all the cards that qualify, so the world answers the whole game so far. The game has **6 rounds (2027 to 2050)**, **78 Scenarios**, **11 Events**, **13 milestones**, **3 tipping points** and **16 Futures**, two hidden arcs (people quietly stop mattering; a tipping point past which the questions are about a world without people), adaptive Events, era drift and flags.

**v5.1 made the game read as one story that climbs.** Every card belongs to **storylines** (its Threads: information, surveillance, work, medicine, minds, race, planet, ai-rule, autonomy) and names in **Follows** the earlier cards, Choices and Events that lead to it. Every earlier decision counts toward the next card, the most recent most, and the game strongly favors the storylines the player has been following (the **spine**), so a game reads like the deepfake → fake video near-war → São Paulo's cameras → California's trust scores → anyone can be named → one network of cameras → Glass Houses. A card never arrives out of nowhere: a card about a subject requires an earlier card that introduced it. **Capability is decided before the card is drawn**: the milestones up to a card's date are worked out first, and a card that assumes AGI, robots or superintelligence only appears in a world that has them by its date (AGI in about 95% of games, two years late in some; superintelligence in about half). Slow-AI worlds get their own cards (the AI bust, rationed power, human checkers, a ceiling treaty, who owns AI's inventions). Only one slowdown milestone is ever shown. The stakes climb: every card has a **Scale** (local, national, bloc, world), later rounds favor larger ones, later Choices move the Values further (Round influence), and the questions grow with them, from what a city or your country should do to what the world should decide. Dates are exact years. Lead-ins tie each card to a specific earlier decision or card (only 1% fall back to a default).

**v5.2 made the player's decisions drive the world's capability, and the Future the natural next step.** The capability curve bends with every Choice (racing steepens it, restraint flattens it), and the milestones' timing follows the Values the player has built: AGI arrives in 2031 in about 64% of games, two years late in about 26%, never in the rest; robots catch up in 2036 only after on-time AGI, otherwise in 2040; the game's first slowdown is always shown, so a card about AI stalling never follows a world that seemed to be racing. Hovering a milestone says **why it happened**, citing the player's own decision and its year ("the cap you signed in 2039"); hovering a card on the strip shows **the Choice made on it**. A Future now needs a **collection** of decisions (two or more, one from round 4 on, or one from the last two rounds), recent ones pull hardest, and the last rounds steer strongly toward the Future the game is heading for (the **leader**), so the final card confirms the trajectory or turns it. How we got here names up to three decisions and never only an early one. The Future is set in **2060**. Eight new cards bridge the gaps the 20-game analysis found (a national and a European AI dividend, who gets the first general AI, discoveries no one can check, AI stalling, machines at the borders, the machines' owners, one AI running the grid).

**v5.3 gave the slow-AI world its own ending and a rhythm in time.** **Back to Earth** is the Future for a world where AI hit a wall: investors stopped getting returns, progress leveled off at the level of a good professional, the boom went bust, and people chose to go back to roughly where they were. A New York finance man who rode the boom tells it in 2060. **No two cards in a row are more than 7 years apart, and neither are the last card and the Future**, so the game moves in a rhythm: dense steps at first (2027, 2029–31, 2034–36), then longer jumps as the stakes rise (2039–41, 2046, 2053), with superintelligence or the plateau in 2040 and the Future in 2060. **Stories match their world.** A Future story can name the world it belongs to (`When`); The Long Middle has a second story for worlds where AI stalled; The Garden and The Oracle, which depend on superintelligence, now require it. **The capability curve flattens for real** after a slowdown (it rises less than 12% of its height afterward, a check), and a new milestone, "AI becomes ordinary" (2048), keeps slow worlds alive. **Events follow the player's storylines** (about 75% of them now), and hovering one on the strip shows "Out of your hands" and a brief summary. The Long Middle's How we got here names the two decisions that pulled the world different ways.

**v5.4 made the world follow the player, with no contradictions.** **AI climbs by default**: every slowing Choice carries a signed **Brake** (racing Choices a negative one), and the milestones follow the balance. AGI arrives on time in about 87% of games, **recursive self-improvement** begins in 2038 in about 78% (its own gauge level, "Self-improving"), and superintelligence follows in 2040 unless the player braked, with at least one global brake. Then AI is **held at the line** by a pause the player called for, or **levels off** because investors stopped paying. A Choice with a Brake of 1 or more either way is a **major Choice**: it bends the capability graph's projection the moment it is made. **The graph moves only with time**: its fill grows during the Interlude and at milestones, never at an Event; the filled part, the past, never changes (a check); recursive self-improvement compounds, so racing worlds look exponential; and in 2060 a sliver of projection remains (Gauge years 2000–2064). **Near misses are seen**: a near war, collapse or runaway is always shown as its own Event, and **devastating Events demand a response**: the very next card answers them. **Versions** (`### Version` with a When) let one card or Event read truly in different worlds instead of writing a second card. Every Future story was read against every Choice: The Last War now requires the war tipping point (AI turns on humanity: war machines, or a bioweapon), and Glass Houses, Two Kinds, Back to Earth, Common Ground, Afterlife, Elsewhere, The Gilded Ones, Blackout and The Oracle are blocked or rewritten wherever they would contradict an explicit Choice. Afterlife, The Pause, A Second Chance and Glass Houses have stories for each world. New: 3 near-miss Events, 3 Events that show AI's growing role (the end of malaria, a solar storm held off autonomously, one faulty update that freezes the world's agents), a card about one switch for every AI, and the milestones "Recursive self-improvement" and "AI held at the line". The game now has **79 Scenarios**, **17 Events**, **15 milestones** and **24 Future stories**. `check-story.js` keeps a table of known contradictions that must never happen, and `--audit` writes a worlds report for finding new ones. The decisions behind all of this are in `docs/review/requirements-audit.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

**v5.5 made AI arrive sooner, and asks the player early how fast it should go.** The calendar and the milestones follow the forecasts of 2026 (`docs/review/v5.5-timelines-audit.md`): rounds are 2027, 2028–29, 2030–34, 2039–41, 2046 and 2053, with **never more than 9 years between cards**. AI does most of the work of building its successor from 2028 (**Assisted AI research**, a new milestone, after OpenAI's automated research intern of September 2026, now on the history Timeline), **AGI** arrives in 2029 (two years late in 2031), true **recursive self-improvement** in 2032 and **superintelligence** in 2035, unless the player paces or stops it. A new flag, **`paced`**, set by a pace Choice, delays self-improvement to 2036 and superintelligence to 2040 (**Self-improvement, step by step** and **Superintelligence, paced**, which Count as the originals) without stopping them; only a pause holds AI at the line (2035), and only several brakes with a global one level it off (2036). Round 1 stays about what the AI of 2027 is already doing. The **pace question** (race, pace or pause) comes in round 2 or 3 of every game: a new card, **r2-assisted** (a London laboratory's AI runs its own research overnight), leads round 2 in about half of games, and in round 3 (**Pace question by round**) the pool narrows to the **Pace cards** if none has been asked. The 140-country treaty (r5-moratorium) and AI designing its own successor (r4-selfimprove) move to 2033 in round 3, so the treaty now comes before superintelligence. r2-datacenter's B is now the pace option. The game has **80 Scenarios**, **17 Events**, **18 milestones** and **24 Future stories**.

**v5.6 made the cards read like news and the results honest.** Every Scenario and Event is written as a New York Times news story (a dated lede, specifics, sourced history, attributed reactions, then the decision), with invented organizations that sound real (recorded in the Names table in `content/CLAUDE.md`). **"As a result" never assumes the future**: it covers about a year after its card, at the card's scale, and `check-story.js` fails a later year or a phrase like "within five years". **Tipping points explain themselves**: the overlay shows the year, the headline and three parts, what happened (plainly, deaths included), why it was this decision (each tipping Choice's **Tipping why**, with what the other Choices would have done) and whether it could have been stopped (the beat's **Prevented** line, naming one of the player's earlier decisions). After a tipping point, **Control, Handover, Unity and Equality stop counting**; the results show them grayed out at a snapshot from just before. **The results show the Values first**, then the Timeline, where each Choice has a pill per Value it moved, struck through if it no longer counted. **The capability graph no longer invents a plateau**: a runaway curve leaves the top of the chart (a chevron marks where), and the gauge's dropdown says what is expected **Ahead** and why, in the player's own decisions. **Strip labels wrap** onto two lines (hyphenated words kept whole), so the desktop strip is 250 px tall. **Every image description** is a snapshot of life in its year, with no robots before they exist (`check-story.js` checks); the approved images whose descriptions changed are listed in `docs/review/v5.6-images-to-regenerate.md`. Overgrowth is now set on an elevated subway platform over the Gowanus Canal in Brooklyn.

**v5.7 made the pace of AI part of the world, and the cards plainer to read.** The engine knows the world's **pace** at any date (racing, paced, held, leveled or beyond), set by the milestones' **Sets pace**, and cards, versions and stories can test it (`pace is held`, `pace not leveled`), so a card written for a racing world never reads in a paused one (r4-factories has a held version). The pace only rises again with a cause on the strip: letting the cap expire (r6-cap/C) or raising it in stages (r6-cap/B, which now sets `paced`) shows **The pause is lifted** or **The cap rises in stages** (2047), and the curve climbs again. Milestones a later card depends on are **Shown: always** (robots catching up, AI helps build AI). While a pause holds, the gauge's level reads **Held at the line**. A curve that would leave the chart now **rescales the whole chart** to fit (the chevron is gone). The **Event panel** glides up to the center when Read more opens and back down on Show less. Going **Home mid-game pauses** the game: Home shows **Continue** and **Restart** instead of Play until the Future is revealed. Text: about a hundred over-compressed sentences were made plainer, news ledes say "on a Monday" rather than "on Monday", and **invented names are in italics** (`*Larkspur*`) and introduced with "a" ("*Larkspur*, a San Francisco research laboratory"), so a reader never takes them for real ones.

**v5.8 made the game calmer and gave the player a collection.** **Milestones arrive in one sweep**: a stretch's milestones are shown during the Interlude as the graph fills once to the next card's year, each diamond landing as the fill passes its year and its caption joining a small stack by the gauge (the strip and gauge stay above the Interlude's dimming). Beside a milestone that moves the gauge, the others now pass quietly (`Shown: always` ones and the first slowdown are still shown). **The tipping beat is slow, like a soft mourning**: longer pauses, no automatic scrolling, clear serif titles over a softer body, "Why your decision tipped the scale", and a wash that breathes. On the strip a tipping point is a **shatter**, not a disc, and as it lands small fragments of the line burst outward and fade. **Events are never cards**: on the results Timeline an Event is a small grey rectangle, like its strip marker, and its hover reads its first two sentences whole ("A magnitude 7.8 earthquake…"). The results drop the separate State of the art section (milestones stay on the Timeline), See what you chose scrolls down to the whole Values table, and a lost Value's pill has one unbroken strike. **Future stories can end by decision**: a story's optional **Ending** lines (like openers) pick its last paragraph, so **Our Legacy, Lost** (formerly The Last War, now a softer dusty rose and "Everything we made outlived us.") closes on what the player did with the archive. **Explore** on Home opens the **Futures collection**: 16 empty frames and a tally ("3/16 discovered"); each Future reached fills its frame with its card, title and tagline, and opens its story as it was read (`localStorage` key `amot-found`). Faint **nebula wisps** drift behind the stars. The About text is Levi's new version, and Future stories are 2 px larger.

**v5.9 made the push for more AI never stop.** Once AI exists, someone always argues for more. In a world **held at the line or leveled off**, seven **Pressure cards** (founders and patients campaigning to leave the pause, generals wanting an exemption, a presidential moonshot after the bust, climate scientists asking for a stronger AI, the cap's renewal, and the public's answer: an antitrust breakup and an election) are met by round 5 (`Pressure question by round`), and two Events (an Izmir earthquake, floods in Bangladesh) show today's capped or stalled AI falling short. The player can hold, **open one field** (medicine, defense or climate: `exempt_*`, "One field opened"), **lift** the line (`lifted`, "The pause is lifted" in 2042, 2047 or 2055) or **restart** the climb after the bust (`relaunch`, "AI climbs again"). Every rise has its Choice on the strip: restraint-held worlds no longer see the pause lifted for no reason (a v5.7 bug), and `r6-cap` now appears in any held world. **The defense carve-out can end the world** without a brilliant AI: fast systems with authority over weapons of mass destruction answer a false alarm (a tip-war Version and a third Our Legacy, Lost story). **The Pause is now the pure world** that kept the whole line, **Back to Earth is rare** (it needs the people's verdict, `people_said_no`), and the carve-out world ends in a third **Long Middle** story (an exemptions reviewer in Vienna). Cards that say AI stalled no longer appear in held worlds. The strip's background now dissolves softly below its lowest label (`--solid-to`). The game has **91 Scenarios**, **19 Events**, **27 milestones** and **29 Future stories**. Four new cards are AI disasters (the Paraná dams, a nerve agent in São Paulo, two airliners over the Gulf, Mumbai's emergency AI), each with "let the system learn from it" as a Choice, and The Long Middle has six stories, one per world, each with its own character and image.

**v6.0 made a decision keep its promise.** A Choice whose words promise an end ("until the world agrees on rules") is now kept or broken on the strip. **The halt** (r2-assisted/C, 2028) is a temporary pause or slow-down that **demands a response** (a Choice can now do what Events did in v5.4: the very next card must answer it, with no Event between), and the next card is always **Geneva** (r3-geneva, 2031, its own card, since its Choices mean something different after a halt), where the *Geneva Compute Treaty* is decided for real. **Sign** and AI is held below general intelligence (no AGI at all, "The pause holds" in 2035); **sign with exemptions** and medicine and defense alone build stronger systems ("Two fields opened", 2036); **refuse** and the race resumes with the three lost years carried over (AGI 2032, self-improvement 2035, superintelligence 2038). r3-geneva's Choices also set `r5-moratorium/A`, `/B` or `/C`, so every later card and Future written for the treaty reads truly. **A worldwide pause sticks**: r4-selfimprove/C now sets `pause` (sota-held tests only `pause`), and `check-story.js` fails any game where AI climbs past a pause without a Choice on the strip that lifted it, raised it in stages, opened a field or restarted the climb. ## What this is

An artistic, interactive web experience about AI and the future, played as a **deck of tarot cards**. It should feel like a piece of art: atmospheric, a little eerie, no visible scoring, thoughtful rather than gamey. It shows the risks and rewards of AI without preaching. No Choice should feel obviously "right", and no Future is purely good or bad.

**The pace is slow and zen.** Nothing pops in or out; everything fades gently. **Treat any visual hiccup as a real bug.**

The player:

0. lands on **Home**: the history Timeline running up to Now above the title (hover or tap an entry for its one line), the capability gauge in the top right corner (hover or tap it for where AI is now), and **Play** and **About** (what the project is, in Levi's words),
1. presses Play: the **Disclaimer** fades in, stays 5 s, and fades out slowly into the game,
2. plays 6 **Rounds** (2027, 2028–29, 2030–34, 2039–41, 2046 and 2053: never more than 9 years between cards), each with its own accent color. Each draws a card from the **deck** and flips it to reveal a dated, news-style **Scenario**: a short **summary** first, with **Read the full story** for the rest. The player picks a **Choice**, reads "As a result…", and the card glides onto the **Timeline strip**. Then, in date order, sometimes an **Event** ("Out of your hands", a panel with **Read more** and **Continue**) and any **milestones** (State of the Art: a diamond on the strip and a box in the Interlude; hovering one says why it happened in this game). Hovering a card on the strip shows the Choice made on it. Then an **Interlude** counts the years up to the next card, shows each milestone it passes, then the card's **opener**, the lead-in sentence to the next card, and waits for **Continue**,
3. on a few of the riskiest Choices, may **tip the world over**: a full-screen **tipping beat** replaces "As a result…" (the year, what happened, why the decision tipped the scale and whether it could have been stopped, slowly), a shatter joins the strip as fragments of the line burst outward, and every later round is a question asked past the point of humanity,
4. draws a final card, the **Future** (set in 2060, seven years after the last card): a title, a tagline, **How we got here** (up to three sentences from the player's own decisions, never only early ones), and the Future's story, with its card image. Everything on the strip slowly takes on the Future's color. A Future is only given if something in the player's own path leads to it,
5. can open **See what you chose**: first the six hidden **Values**, which explain themselves (after a tipping point, the four that stopped counting are grayed out at where they stood just before it, and say why), then their Timeline from Now to 2060 (which also recolors to the Future), where each Choice shows a pill per Value it moved and every milestone they reached has its place (v5.8: no separate State of the art section).

**The present year is 2026.** Anywhere the UI means "now" or "today", it means 2026.

## Content lives in `site/content/story.md`

**Every word players see is in `site/content/story.md`**: UI strings, the Disclaimer, About, the history Timeline, the Values, all Scenarios, Events, milestones, tipping beats and Futures, Timeline labels, openers, source links, image descriptions, the image style guide and every tunable setting. The game `fetch`es and parses it on every page load, so edits show up on reload with no build step. **Don't put content in code**: the engine holds only logic, and the UI holds only layout.

After editing it, run **`node tools/check-story.js`**. It must report no problems, no chronology problems, no style problems, no ungrounded required flags and none of the "never" checks (see Checks). **`node tools/check-story.js --audit`** also writes `docs/path-audit.md`, sample playthroughs per Future and of both arcs, for a person to read. **`node tools/make-review-docs.js`** rebuilds Levi's two Word review documents in `docs/review/` from the current content.

`archive/content/` holds things kept but not used: `future-stories.md` (every Future story taken out of the game in v5, word for word, ready to paste back), `old-root-story.md` (an old copy of the content) and `make_doc.js` (the script behind the first futures review). The game never reads it.

### Running it

`fetch` doesn't work from `file://`, so the game needs a local server:

- **`tools/start.command`** (double-click): `cd`s to the repo, starts `tools/serve.py` on port 8000 (or the next free port), and opens the game in the default browser.
- Or run `python3 tools/serve.py 8000` from the repo and open `http://localhost:8000`.
- `tools/serve.py` serves **`site/` only**, sends `Cache-Control: no-cache`, listens on 127.0.0.1 only, answers only requests addressed to localhost, and never serves hidden files. A plain `python3 -m http.server --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory site` is also safe now, since `site/` holds only public files; never run one from the repo root, which would serve `image-studio/.env`.
- Opened straight from Finder, the page shows "To play, double-click start.command in the game's tools folder." That one sentence is in `site/index.html`, because it has to work when `story.md` can't load.

## Writing rules

All player-facing text follows the Writing rules: at the top of `story.md` and in `docs/content.md` (no colons, semicolons, em or en dashes; American spelling; NYT register; source every real fact; never invent a source). To add a card, use the `add-card` skill. The `story.md` format, the engine and the UI flow are documented in `docs/content.md` and `docs/engine.md`.

## Testing

**Run `node tools/play-test.js` after any change to the game's code or layout; a change isn't done until it passes.** Pass bar: **1,000 desktop games with zero failures** (`node tools/play-test.js --games 1000 --viewport desktop`, about 75 minutes, wrap in `caffeinate -i`), plus a look through `--shots` screenshots of at least five games (including a tipping point and two replays). Then 300 phone games; phone failures are reported but don't block a release. Test hooks (`?seed=N`, `?test=1`, `window.__game`) are in `docs/engine.md`; harness options and failure reproduction are in the `play-test` skill.

## Terminology (use these exact names in code and comments)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Disclaimer** | The screen after Play: fades in, holds 5 s, fades out slowly, then the game begins |
| **Timeline** | The history of AI that ends at Now, shown on Home as a horizontal line |
| **Now** | Where history stops and the game begins: "Now · 2026" |
| **Deck** | The pile of card backs during the game: one per Round (6), plus the Future |
| **Timeline strip** | The thin, chronological Timeline across the top during play; each card lands there as a tiny card, each Event as a gray one, each milestone as a diamond, a tipping point as a small shatter (v5.8) |
| **Round** | One decision step (6 per game, 2027 to 2050), each with its own **round color**, **round years** and **round scale** |
| **Scenario** / **card** | A dated news story: a Summary, the full Story on request, a question and 3 Choices |
| **Storylet** | How cards are chosen: each card declares its **Round**, its **Appears when** conditions and its **Openers**; no card links to another |
| **Pool** | Every card that qualifies at the start of a round |
| **Continuity** | Preferring a card whose **Follows** list names decisions this game made, the most recent counting most |
| **Storyline** / **spine** | A card's Threads are its storylines; the spine is the storylines the player's decisions have dwelt on, recent ones most. Cards on the spine are strongly favored |
| **Scale** | How far a card's consequences reach (local, national, bloc, world); each round has a minimum (Round scales) |
| **Opener** | A lead-in sentence on a card, shown in the Interlude before it: the one tied to the most recent decision whose condition holds, else its **default** |
| **Flag** | A fact about the game so far. Every Choice sets `<card>/<Letter>` and `<card>`, every Event and tipping beat its id; Choices can set named flags (`pause`, `watched`, `enhanced`, `restraint`, `tends_life`); the engine sets `agents_era`, `near_miss`, `near_war`, `near_collapse`, `near_runaway`, `brake_world`, `after_humanity`, `tended_life`; a Choice can end a named flag (`Clears: pause`) |
| **Choice** / **Consequence** | One of the 3 answers to a Scenario / its "As a result…" text |
| **Event** | Something the player doesn't control ("Out of your hands"), a **panel** between rounds, never a card: AI meeting a disaster too large for people alone, a broad benefit, or a vulnerability no one considered. Adaptive: each has a **Likelihood**, raised when it's on the player's storylines and lowered when it isn't; hovering its strip marker shows a brief summary. One that **Demands response** is answered by the very next card |
| **Milestone** | A State of the Art item: a moment in AI capability. Never a card; moves the gauge and stamps the strip. Items sharing a **Slot** are alternatives, chosen by **Condition**. A **slowdown** (Progress steadies, The pause holds, progress levels off, meets its limits) is shown at most once per game |
| **Futures collection** (v5.8) | Explore on Home: every Future as an empty frame until reached, then its card, title and tagline; clicking one opens its story. Kept in `localStorage` (`amot-found`) |
| **Capability gauge** | The area graph at the top right: AI capability from 2000 to 2064, filled to the year the game has reached (only as years pass; the filled past never changes), bent by the milestones reached and by **major Choices** (a Brake of 1 or more either way), which reshape only the projection. A runaway curve rescales the chart so all of it fits (v5.7). Its dropdown shows the latest milestone and what is expected **Ahead** |
| **Interlude** | The screen between cards (v6.1): a large year counting up to the next card, a box for each milestone it passes, the opener, and Continue |
| **Values** | The 6 hidden numbers each Choice, Event and milestone changes; **scaled** wherever they're tested |
| **Era drift** | Small nudges to the Values at the start of some rounds, because the technology advances on its own |
| **Pressure cards** (v5.9) | SETTINGS → Pressure cards: the cards where someone pushes for more AI in a world held or leveled off, or the public pushes back. From round `Pressure question by round` (5), a held or leveled game that hasn't met one narrows to them. Their Choices can set `exempt_medicine`, `exempt_defense`, `exempt_climate` (one field opened), `lifted`, `relaunch` or `people_said_no` |
| **Pace** (v5.7) | The world's pace at a date: racing, paced, held, leveled or beyond, set by milestones' **Sets pace** and tested with `pace is held` / `pace not held` |
| **Pace cards** / **paced** | The cards that ask how fast AI should go, with a race, a pace and a pause or slow-down Choice (SETTINGS → Pace cards); by round `Pace question by round` (3) one has been asked, or the pool narrows to them. A pace Choice sets `paced`: self-improvement and superintelligence come four and five years later (2036, 2040), never stopped |
| **Brake balance** / **brake_world** | Hidden: slowing Choices and Events add to it (`Brake: +1`), racing Choices take from it (`Brake: -1`); a positive one on a world-scale card, a pause or restraint also sets `brake_world`. Milestone conditions test `brakes` (a slowdown in 2040 needs 2 and a global brake) |
| **Agents counter** / **agents era** | Hidden: Choices with `Agents: +1` add to it; at 2 (from round 4) the flag `agents_era` is set and agents cards (those that require `agents_era`) take over the later rounds |
| **Tipping point** / **tipping beat** | A risky Choice made when the world is near the edge tips it over; the beat replaces "As a result…", and later rounds draw only **After humanity** cards. The beat has a Text (with Versions), the Choice's **Tipping why** and the beat's **Prevented** line (v5.6) |
| **Lost values** | SETTINGS → Values lost at the tipping point (Control, Handover, Unity, Equality): after a tipping point their changes stop counting, and the results show them grayed out at `state.snapshot` |
| **Near miss** | A tipping Choice made close to the edge but not over it: the flags `near_miss` and `near_war`, `near_collapse` or `near_runaway`, and its **Near miss Event**, always shown, answered by the next card |
| **Version** | A `### Version` of a card or Event with a **When**: the text it shows in a world where the When holds, so one card reads truly in different worlds |
| **Future** | One of the 16 endings, drawn as the final card (2060). Each has Targets, Requires, Grounded by and a story; it's given only when a collection of the player's decisions leads to it |
| **Grounded by** / **How we got here** | A Future's list of the decisions, Events and tipping beats that can lead to it / the one or two of its sentences shown at the reveal |
| **Steering** | From round 4, among cards that continue the story, preferring a card whose Choices ground the **leader** (the Future the game is heading for), more strongly each round (`Steering bonus` per round) |
| **Leader** / **Pull** | The Futures ranked by distance minus `Future pull` × pull (each grounding decision counts `Future pull decay` ^ rounds ago), only those whose required flags are set / how strongly the game's decisions point to a Future |
| **Because** | A milestone's lines `- condition \| sentence`, chosen like openers; the sentence shown on hover says why it happened, with `{year}` filled with the year the player made that decision |
| **Recolor** | At the end, the strip and the results Timeline slowly take on the Future's accent |

## Text colors and contrast

Color tokens live on `:root` in `styles.css`; use them everywhere. **Nothing a player needs to read is darker than `--text-2`**; `--text-faint` is decorative only, never for words. Accent-colored text must reach **4.5:1**; `readable(color, bg)` mixes the color toward `--text` until it passes.

## Cards

- Card art is **9:16** with square corners; **all corner rounding is CSS**: radius = 1/32 of the card's width (`calc(100cqw / 32)` on each face).
- Cards use their `Image` when set (for a Future, the story's `Image`). Otherwise `setFace()` draws the **placeholder face** in HTML and CSS (`.ph`): a dark card with a thin cream frame, a dashed inner line, faint hatching, an hourglass, "Placeholder" and a note that the art is still being made. **For launch every card front is the placeholder.** Batch 1 (the 5 openers and the 9 original Futures' Story 1) has art, kept in `image-studio/published/`: each of those cards has an empty `**Image:**` and, under it, `**Archived image:** images/cards/<id>.webp` (the parser ignores that field). **To bring the art back:** `git mv image-studio/published/*.webp site/images/cards/` and copy each Archived image path into the Image field above it (delete the Archived line). `node tools/build.js` fails if `site/images/cards/` holds an image no Image field uses, so archived art can't slip back in by accident. The card back (`site/images/cards/card-back.webp`) is always shown.

### Image studio (`image-studio/`; never shipped)

Card images are made **ahead of time** by Levi's private tool in `image-studio/`, following `archive/briefs/v4-images.md` and `archive/briefs/v4-images-addendum-nano-banana.md`. **It is never shipped**: it is outside `site/`, nothing in the game links to it, and `tools/build.js` fails if any of it is in `site/`. The API keys live only in `image-studio/.env` (git-ignored, mode 600), read only by its local Python scripts. Its Nano Banana provider is the default; images get Levi's border frame; `review.py` (localhost:8765) lets him approve, deny or regenerate with notes; `publish.py` writes approved cards to `site/images/cards/<id>.webp` and fills the card's `**Image:**` field. **Never call it, or any paid API, without Levi.** (Details: the v4 briefs and the scripts' own headers.)

## Deploying

Cloudflare Pages deploys **`main`** (production branch `main`, build command `node tools/build.js`, output directory `site`) to levirain.com. `site/` is published as it is. `node tools/build.js` copies nothing: it is the **site check**, and it fails (so Cloudflare won't deploy) if `site/` holds anything private (`image-studio/`, `tools/`, docs, reviews, archives, tests, `node_modules/`, any `.env` or other hidden file, Markdown other than `content/story.md`, scripts, documents or logs), a card image no Image field uses, or misses a file the game needs (including `404.html`). Run it before every commit to `launch`. `main` is pushed only by operation spirit rain (above). `dist/` is no longer used.
