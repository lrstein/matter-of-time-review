## r1-frontier
**Added:** v5.5
**Headline:** In San Francisco, an AI Now Writes Most of the Code for Its Own Successor
**Timeline label:** AI writes its successor
**Date:** 2027
**Place:** San Francisco
**Round:** 1
**Threads:** ai-rule, race, autonomy
**Scale:** national
**Weight:** 3
**Image:**

**Summary:** A San Francisco laboratory says its AI now writes most of the code for the next version of itself, and each version arrives in months rather than years. A rival laboratory in Beijing is thought to be close behind. Congress must decide how fast American laboratories may go.

**Story:**
On a Tuesday morning in San Francisco, one of the world's leading AI laboratories publishes a short report about its own engineers. Most of the code in its newest system was written by the system before it. The engineers no longer write much themselves. They decide what to try, read what the AI has built and choose which of its experiments to keep. The next version, the laboratory says, will arrive in months, not years.

The change did not come from nowhere. In September 2026, [OpenAI said it had built an automated research intern](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/), a system that carries out well-defined research tasks under human direction. A year later, the intern has become most of the staff.

A rival laboratory in Beijing is thought to be only a few months behind, and its government has said it will not slow down for anyone.

In Washington, the hearings turn on two warnings. The laboratories say that whichever country slows down will lose the lead, perhaps for good. A growing number of their own researchers say that no one can properly check work done this fast, and that each new version will be harder to understand than the last.

**Question:** How fast should America's AI laboratories be allowed to go?

### Choice A
**Text:** Let them go as fast as they can. Beijing will not wait.
**Effects:** power +2, handover +1, control -1, unity -1
**Brake:** -1
**As a result:** The laboratories double their computing within a year, and each new version of AI helps build the next a little faster. Beijing answers with its own national program. Safety researchers warn that testing has fallen months behind the systems it is meant to test.

### Choice B
**Text:** Let each new version out only after independent testers have checked it, and let computing grow on a fixed schedule.
**Effects:** control +1, unity +1, power -1
**Brake:** +1
**Sets:** paced
**As a result:** Every new system now waits a few weeks for outside testers before it is released, and the laboratories plan their growth years in advance. They complain that Beijing faces no such rules. The testers find two serious flaws in the first year, and the laboratories fix both before release.

### Choice C
**Text:** Stop training anything more powerful, and ask Beijing to stop too.
**Effects:** control +2, unity +1, power -2, equality -1
**Brake:** +2
**Sets:** restraint
**As a result:** The American laboratories halt their largest training runs, and several of their best researchers leave for laboratories abroad. Beijing says it will consider a mutual pause, and keeps building. The rest of the world watches to see who blinks.
