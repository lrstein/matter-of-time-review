## What v3.5 is about

In playtesting, the ending sometimes felt unearned. One player went from a power grid flaw to Texas power cuts, Estonia's AI vote, the collapse of the AI weapons talks and a runaway AI, and then landed in **Elsewhere**, a future about people escaping into virtual worlds. Nothing in that path led there. v3.5 fixes this in three ways:

1. **Futures must be grounded in the player's own path.** A player can only receive a Future if at least one decision or Event in their game is listed as leading to it.
2. **The reveal shows how they got there.** One or two sentences, drawn from the player's actual decisions, appear before the story.
3. **The last rounds steer toward the ending.** In rounds 3 and 4, the game prefers cards that can lead to the Futures the player is closest to.

v3.5 also adds **State of the Art** cards. These are milestones such as cheaper chips, AGI and superintelligence that keep the player grounded in how capable AI has become.

## 2. Choosing the Future (changed)

1. Rank Futures by distance to the player's final Values, closest first.
2. The player receives the first Future that is within the threshold (3.0), meets its `Requires`, **and has at least one `Grounded by` entry that actually happened in this game**. That means the player chose that exact choice on that card, or that Event appeared.
3. Otherwise, the player receives The Long Middle.

## 3. Steering the final rounds

In **rounds 3 and 4**, when ranking the two candidate cards, first prefer a card whose choices appear in the `Grounded by` list of either of the player's **two nearest Futures**. Then fall back to the existing explore (round 3) or tiebreaker (round 4) score. Keep the `Best-fit pick chance` (0.6), so there is still variety.

In simulation, for most Futures, more than 80% of games that reach them are grounded by a round 3 or round 4 decision. The ending usually follows from the player's most recent, highest-stakes choices.

## 4. The reveal shows how they got there

After the Future's title and tagline, before the story:

- Show a small label using `Looking back label` ("How we got here").
- Beneath it, show the matching `Grounded by` sentences from this game. If there are several, show **two**, the earliest and the latest, in chronological order. If there's only one, show one.
- Fade them in slowly, one after the other, in a slightly smaller italic, then continue into the story paragraphs as before.

These two sentences are the bridge between the player's choices and the life they're about to read about, so give them room.

## 5. State of the Art cards (new)

- **When they appear.** After the player's choice in round 1, 2 or 3 (and after any Event), check the State of the Art items whose `Shown after round` matches.
  - Skip any item whose `Condition` the player's current Values don't meet.
  - Items that share a `Slot` are alternatives. Show only the first one in file order whose condition is met. For example, `sota-agi` appears if Power is 0 or higher, and otherwise `sota-steady` appears.
  - Apply each shown item's Effects.
- **Display: card.**
  - Draw and flip it like other cards, with the label `State of the art` (from UI TEXT) above the headline.
  - Show three short sections with small headings, "What happened", "What it means" and "How life changes", then a **Continue** button only.
  - Give it its own quiet visual identity, distinct from both Scenarios and Events. A cool, muted tint such as `#9fb4c7` works well.
- **Display: point.** No card. Add a small marker to the timeline at its date with its label, fading in during the next transition.
- **On the timeline.** Every State of the Art item that appeared gets a marker at its date with its `Timeline label` (for example "AGI achieved"), tinted `#9fb4c7` as it lands, using the same placement rules as other markers. It also appears in "See what you chose".
- **Order within a transition:** "As a result…", then any Event, then any State of the Art card, then the time-gap and lead-in interstitial, then the next card.

## 6. The top timeline strip leaves at the end

On the Future reveal, once the player scrolls past the end of the story and the Future card begins moving up toward "See what you chose", the top timeline strip should **move up and fade out at the same rate**, tied to scroll position. It should be fully gone before the "See what you chose" timeline comes into view, since that timeline shows everything the strip did and more. If the player scrolls back up, the strip returns the same way.
